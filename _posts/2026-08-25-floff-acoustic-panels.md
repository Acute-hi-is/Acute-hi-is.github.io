ACUTE recently collaborated with **[Flöff](https://www.floff.is/)**, an Icelandic startup working with recycled textiles, to characterise the acoustic properties of the materials behind their colourful sound panels.

Ever-growing textile waste is a mounting global problem, and creative reuse is not only eco-friendly but also an economic and material solution. Flöff saves textiles from landfill and gives them a new purpose: colourful acoustic panels that absorb sound while diverting waste from disposal.

![Flöff's recycled-textile discs alongside the impedance tube](/images/posts/floff/floff-impedance-tube.jpg)
*Flöff's recycled-textile samples with the Brüel & Kjær Type 4206-T impedance tube.*

Using the lab's new **[Brüel & Kjær Type 4206-T impedance tube](/facilities/impedance-tube/)**, we measured the sound-absorption characteristics of the reused materials Flöff is currently manufacturing. This industry-standard equipment provided accurate data on the panels' performance, giving Flöff's team the insight they needed to refine the design and offer better acoustic quality to future customers.

![Acoustic testing in progress in the lab](/images/posts/floff/floff-in-action.jpg)
*Measuring the panels' acoustic performance in the lab.*

The collaboration is a small example of what ACUTE's facilities are for — [our acoustic, audio, and fabrication infrastructure is open](/facilities/) to companies, visiting researchers, and artists who need precise measurement for their own work.
